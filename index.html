Writing

The Ruthless Editor

Tighten a draft without losing your voice.

Act as a sharp line editor. Here is my draft for [audience] whose goal is [goal]:
[paste draft]
1. Cut it by about [20]% without removing any facts or examples.
2. Keep my voice: I tend to write [casual/formal/wry]; don't make it sound corporate.
3. Return the edited version, then a bullet list of the 5 biggest changes and why.
Do not add new claims I didn't make.

Example: Original: "I just wanted to reach out and let everyone know that we are going to be moving the meeting." → Edited: "Heads up: the meeting is moving." Change: removed throat-clearing ("just wanted to reach out"); led with the news.

Tip: Give a target word count instead of a percentage for tight limits. Ask it to flag sentences it was unsure about cutting.

Headline Tasting Flight

Generate and rank headlines for a post, email, or video.

Write 12 headlines for [piece of content: one-sentence summary] aimed at [audience].
Use 4 styles, 3 each: (a) plain benefit, (b) specific number, (c) curiosity without clickbait, (d) contrarian.
Max [60] characters each. Then pick your top 3 and explain in one line why each would get clicked by this audience specifically.

Example: (b) "7 Prompts That Cut My Email Time in Half" · (d) "Stop Asking AI to 'Make It Better'" · Top pick: (d) - it names a habit the reader recognizes and promises a fix.

Tip: Paste 2-3 headlines that performed well for you before so it can match your style.

Explain It Like a Friend

Turn jargon-heavy text into plain English.

Rewrite the text below so a smart [12-year-old / non-expert in field] understands it on first read.
Rules: sentences under 20 words, define any unavoidable term in brackets the first time, use one everyday analogy, and keep every factual point.
Text: [paste]

Example: "APR is the yearly cost of borrowing [the interest plus fees, shown as a percent]. Think of it as the price tag on a loan: two loans of the same size can have very different price tags."

Tip: Ask for a reading-level estimate before and after to confirm it actually got simpler.

Story Spine Builder

Outline a short story, talk, or brand story.

Use the story-spine structure (Once upon a time / Every day / Until one day / Because of that x3 / Until finally / Ever since) to outline a [story type] about [subject].
Tone: [tone]. Length when written: about [words].
Give the spine, then 3 alternative "Until one day" turning points that would change the story's direction.

Example: Once upon a time, a bakery owner did every order by hand. Every day she missed a few. Until one day a wedding cake order slipped... Because of that she tried a simple checklist...

Tip: Works great for About pages: use your own business history as the subject.

Work & Email

The Diplomatic No

Decline a request while protecting the relationship.

Write a short email declining [request] from [person, relationship, e.g. my manager / a client].
Reason (be honest but brief): [reason].
Offer one alternative: [alternative or 'suggest one'].
Tone: warm, firm, no over-apologizing. Under 120 words. Give 2 versions: one more formal, one more casual.

Example: "Thanks for thinking of me for the Q3 report. I can't take it on this month without delaying the launch work, which I want to do well. I could review a draft on the 20th if that helps."

Tip: Remove any 'sorry' it adds more than once; one apology is plenty.

Meeting Notes to Actions

Turn messy notes or a transcript into a follow-up.

Here are raw notes from a meeting about [topic]:
[paste notes]
Produce: 1) 3-sentence summary, 2) decisions made, 3) action items as a table (owner | task | due date; write 'unassigned' or 'no date' if missing), 4) open questions.
Don't invent owners or dates.

Example: | Owner | Task | Due | | Dana | Send revised quote | Fri | | unassigned | Confirm venue capacity | no date |

Tip: The 'don't invent' line matters; check the 'unassigned' items and fix them before sending.

Reply to the Angry Email

Respond calmly to a frustrated customer or coworker.

Draft a reply to this email: [paste].
My goals: acknowledge the specific frustration, state what I can actually do ([what you can do]), and set a next step with a date.
Avoid: blaming, defensive phrasing, and generic lines like 'we value your feedback'. Under 150 words.

Example: "You're right that waiting two weeks with no update isn't okay. I've escalated your refund and will email you by Thursday with a confirmation number."

Tip: Wait 10 minutes and reread before sending. Edit the commitments so they're ones you can keep.

Weekly Status in 5 Minutes

Write a skimmable weekly update for your boss or team.

Turn these bullet notes into a weekly status update for [audience]: [paste notes].
Format: 🟢 Done, 🟡 In progress (with % or next step), 🔴 Blocked (with what I need and from whom). Top line: one-sentence overall status.
Keep each bullet under 15 words.

Example: Overall: Launch on track; one blocker on legal review. 🔴 Terms page - need sign-off from Legal by Wed.

Tip: Keep a running notes file all week and paste it on Friday.

Small Business

Customer Avatar Interview

Get clear on who you sell to.

Interview me to build a customer profile for my [business type] that sells [product/service] in [location/online].
Ask one question at a time, 8 questions max, about who buys, why, what they compared, and what nearly stopped them.
After my last answer, write a one-paragraph customer profile and 3 phrases they'd actually say that I can use in marketing.

Example: Profile: "Busy parents in suburban Austin who want a birthday cake that looks custom but can be ordered in under 5 minutes..." Phrases: "I don't have time to go back and forth."

Tip: Answer with real customer quotes from reviews when you can.

Google Business Profile Glow-Up

Write a local listing description and posts.

Write a Google Business Profile description (max 750 characters) for [business name], a [type] in [city/neighborhood].
Include: what we do, who it's for, 2 specific differentiators ([list]), and a call to action. Mention the neighborhood naturally once, no keyword stuffing.
Then write 3 short update posts (under 300 characters each) for the next month.

Example: "Family-run bike shop in East Nashville fixing commuter and kids' bikes, most repairs done same day..."

Tip: Only list differentiators that are true and verifiable; local customers will check.

Price Increase Announcement

Tell customers about a price change without losing them.

Write a message to existing customers announcing a price change from [old] to [new] for [product/service], effective [date].
Reason: [honest reason]. Something we're adding or keeping: [value].
Tone: straightforward and grateful. Include an option for loyal customers: [e.g. lock in old price until date]. Under 170 words, for [email/text/social].

Example: "Starting March 1, our monthly lawn plan goes from $45 to $52. Fuel and equipment costs rose, and we'd rather adjust than cut corners..."

Tip: Give at least 30 days notice; send it before the bill, not with it.

Competitor Snapshot

Compare yourself to competitors from public info.

I'll paste public info (website text, reviews) from [2-3 competitors] and my business.
[paste]
Build a comparison table: offer, price signals, who they target, top praise in reviews, top complaint in reviews.
Then suggest 3 gaps I could own, based only on what's in the text. Mark anything that's a guess as (guess).

Example: Gap: "Two competitors get repeated complaints about no-show appointments; offering text reminders and a guaranteed arrival window could be your hook."

Tip: Reviews are gold: paste the 1- and 3-star ones, not just the 5-stars.

Learning

Socratic Tutor

Learn a topic by being questioned, not lectured.

Be my Socratic tutor for [topic]. My current level: [beginner/some basics/etc.].
Don't give answers directly. Ask me one question at a time, starting easy. If I'm wrong, give a hint, not the answer. After every 5 questions, summarize what I've shown I understand and what's still shaky.

Example: Tutor: "If you put $100 in an account that earns 10% a year, how much is there after one year?" Me: "$110." Tutor: "Right. Now what happens in year two: does it earn $10 again, or something else?"

Tip: Say 'switch to explain mode' if you're stuck for 3 rounds.

30-Day Skill Plan

Build a realistic learning schedule.

Create a 30-day plan to learn [skill] to the point where I can [concrete outcome].
I have [minutes] per day, on [days]. Resources I already have: [list or 'none'].
Each week: a goal, daily tasks (under my time limit), and a small project to prove progress. Include 2 rest/catch-up days per week.

Example: Week 1 goal: Hold a 2-minute conversation introducing yourself in Spanish. Day 3 (20 min): learn 15 'about me' phrases; record yourself saying them.

Tip: A concrete outcome ('order dinner in Spanish') beats a vague one ('learn Spanish').

Flashcard Factory

Turn notes or a chapter into study cards.

Turn the text below into [20] flashcards in a table (Front | Back).
Mix types: definitions, 'why' questions, and 'what would happen if' questions. Backs under 25 words. Skip trivial facts.
Text: [paste]

Example: Front: "Why does a vaccine need a booster?" | Back: "Immune memory can fade; a booster re-exposes the body so it rebuilds strong, fast antibody responses."

Tip: Paste the table into Anki or Quizlet using their import from CSV/tab option.

Steelman Both Sides

Understand a debate fairly.

Explain the debate over [issue]. Give the strongest version of each main position (steelman, not strawman), the best evidence each side points to, and where they actually disagree (values vs. facts).
End with 3 questions I could ask to figure out where I stand. Stay neutral.

Example: "Much of the disagreement on remote work is about values (flexibility vs. in-person culture) rather than facts, since productivity studies point both ways depending on the job."

Tip: Ask for sources and verify them; AI can misremember studies.

Planning & Life

Weekly Meal Plan from the Fridge

Plan meals around what you already have.

Plan [5] dinners for [number] people using mostly these ingredients I already have: [list].
Constraints: [dietary needs], max [30] minutes active cooking, budget about [amount] for extra groceries.
Give: the plan, a combined shopping list grouped by store section, and one prep step I can do Sunday to save time.

Example: Tue: Sheet-pan chicken thighs with the half bag of carrots and potatoes (25 min). Sunday prep: chop all onions for Mon–Wed.

Tip: List what's about to expire first so it gets used early in the week.

Trip Itinerary Draft

Draft a day-by-day trip plan.

Draft a [days]-day itinerary for [destination] in [month] for [who's going, interests].
Pace: [relaxed/moderate/packed]. Budget level: [budget]. Group nearby sights together to reduce travel, include one rainy-day backup each day, and note anything that typically needs advance booking.
Format as a table per day: morning / afternoon / evening.

Example: Day 2 (Old Town, all walkable): Morning: castle (book ahead in summer) · Afternoon: food market · Evening: river walk. Rainy backup: history museum.

Tip: Verify hours and prices on official sites; AI info can be out of date.

Decision Matrix

Make a hard choice with less second-guessing.

Help me decide between [options]. What matters to me: [criteria].
First ask me to weight each criterion 1-5. Then score each option 1-10 per criterion with one-line reasoning, show the weighted totals, and tell me which result would surprise me and why that's worth noticing.

Example: Weighted total: Apartment A 7.8, Apartment B 7.1. "If you feel disappointed A won, commute time may matter more to you than you weighted it."

Tip: Your gut reaction to the result is data. Use the matrix to understand it, not override it.

Budget Reset

Make a simple monthly budget from real numbers.

Here's my monthly take-home pay: [amount] and my spending by category last month: [list].
Suggest a realistic budget for next month that reaches [goal, e.g. save $300]. Show categories in a table (last month | proposed | change), point out the 3 easiest cuts, and don't cut [things I won't change].
This is for planning, not financial advice.

Example: | Dining out | $420 | $300 | -$120 | Easiest cut: two fewer takeout nights per week.

Tip: Export last month's transactions from your bank to get accurate numbers.

Coding Basics

Explain This Code

Understand code you didn't write.

Explain this [language] code to someone who knows [your level].
[paste code]
Give: 1) one-sentence purpose, 2) a line-by-line (or block-by-block) walkthrough, 3) any bugs or risky parts, 4) one question I should ask whoever wrote it.

Example: Purpose: "Reads a CSV and totals sales by month." Risk: "Line 12 assumes every row has a date; a blank row will crash it."

Tip: Remove passwords and API keys before pasting code anywhere.

Error Message Decoder

Fix an error without guessing.

I got this error: [paste full error].
Running: [what you ran], on [OS / tool versions]. Relevant code: [paste].
Explain what the error means in plain English, list the 3 most likely causes ranked, and give the exact fix for the most likely one. Then tell me how to confirm it worked.

Example: "ModuleNotFoundError: Python can't find 'requests'. Most likely: it isn't installed in the environment you're running. Fix: `pip install requests`, then rerun."

Tip: Always paste the full error, including the last lines. That's where the cause usually is.

Spreadsheet Formula Helper

Get the right Excel/Google Sheets formula.

In [Google Sheets/Excel], my data is: column A = [..], B = [..], C = [..], rows 2 to [n].
I want to [goal, e.g. sum C where A is 'Paid' and B is in March].
Give the formula, explain each part, and show the result on these 3 sample rows: [paste].

Example: =SUMIFS(C2:C200, A2:A200, "Paid", B2:B200, ">="&DATE(2026,3,1), B2:B200, "<"&DATE(2026,4,1))

Tip: Describe columns by header names, not just letters, to avoid mix-ups.

Build a Tiny Script

Automate a small repetitive task.

Write a beginner-friendly [Python] script that [task, e.g. renames all photos in a folder to date_taken.jpg].
I'm on [OS]. Include: install steps, comments on every block, a dry-run mode that only prints what it would do, and how to run it.
Keep it under [60] lines with no unnecessary libraries.

Example: `python rename.py --dry-run` → "IMG_2043.jpg -> 2026-05-14_1.jpg (not renamed, dry run)"

Tip: Always run the dry run first and back up the folder.
